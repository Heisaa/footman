# The vocabulary

What the words mean — the ones in the diagnostic tables, the code comments and
`PLAN.md`. Several are ordinary football words used in a narrow technical sense,
and a few pairs read as synonyms and are not.

Where a term is really a mechanism, this file says what it is and names the file
that owns it. `docs/DIAGNOSTICS.md` says which instrument can see it.

Read "Words that are not synonyms" first if you read nothing else. Every pair
there has caused a misreading of a diagnostic.

---

## What a player does with the ball

Every touch in the match is one of these. They are `SimTelemetry.Touch` in the
event log and `SimDecision.Action` in the decision layer. The two lists do not
line up exactly: a **hold** is an action but not a touch kind, because it is
played as a small dribble.

**Touch** — any contact with the ball, by anyone, for any reason. The unit the
event log counts. A player is allowed one every `touch_cooldown_length()`, which
shortens with technique. A carry runs a good deal slower than that ceiling, because
he has to catch the ball up first. `Ball control` reports the measured interval.

**On-ball decision** — the moment a player in contact with the ball is asked what
to do. `SimDecision.choose_and_execute`. Every option is generated, scored, and one
is picked by softmax. Roughly one per touch.

**Carry** — moving the ball along with you, in a series of touches. Not a single
event: the log holds a run of `dribble` touches by the same player, and `Ball
control` reports their rhythm — time between, distance the ball runs. "Carry" and
"dribble" are used interchangeably in the code.

**Dribble** — one touch of a carry. Generated by `_add_dribbles` as eight probes
around the compass, each scored on where it puts the ball, whether the man closing
gets there first, and whether the ball stays on the field.

**Hold** — a settling touch: the ball stays where it is. Scored as keeping
possession and advancing nothing (`_add_hold` reads its value at the player's own
feet), and executed by `_play_hold` as the smallest touch the engine has, aimed by
`_safe_direction`. **It is played as a `dribble` touch in the log**, so a hold and a
carry are indistinguishable there. That is how three quarters of the carries in a
match went unexamined for a long time; see `docs/STATUS.md`, "The hold that was a
carry".

**Knock past a man**, also **burst** — the long one. A carrier at pace pushes the
ball nine metres in front and turns it into a foot race, rather than taking another
touch under his sole. A separate candidate from the carry, with its own gate: below
`BURST_PACE` it is not a foot race, it is giving the ball away. Appears in the
diagnostics as `knock on`.

**First touch** — taking down a ball that arrives with pace on it. A different
primitive from a dribble (`SimTouch.first_touch`): it has an incoming ball to kill,
it can only turn so far in one touch, and it is graded by `quality`.

**Clearance** — hitting it away, long and wide, because keeping it is not on. Its
value is scored as the threat it *removes*, not the threat it creates.

**Poke** — a defender's touch when he wins the ball but cannot control it: a
scramble with a loosely intended direction. Logged as `tackle` or `block`.

**Pass** — four kinds, and which ones exist at all is decided by distance rather
than by weighting. **Ground pass** along the floor; **lofted pass** in the air;
**through ball**, played into space for a runner rather than to feet; **cross**,
into the box from wide. Below `LOFTED_FROM` the only pass on offer is along the
ground, past `MAX_GROUND_PASS` only the air, and in between both are generated and
the softmax chooses.

**Shot** — struck at the goal. Scored in the same currency as everything else:
`expected_goals` times one goal.

**Header** — a ball played above `SimAerial.HEADER_FROM`, which is the shoulders:
below it he has a chest. Three intents, stamped on the touch so the log can tell
them apart — **cleared**, **at goal**, and **to a man**, the knock-down. A header
at goal is logged as a shot, because from outside it is one.

**Chest** — a ball between the boot and the shoulders, killed and put on the
grass in front of him (`SimTouch.chest`). The same skill, difficulty and dice as
a first touch, with a tighter cushion and no lift: what it buys is the ball at
his feet a moment later rather than ground now. It is the commonest thing done
with a ball in the air, and it is what stops every bounce being a header.

**Letting it drop** — declining to head a ball at all
(`SimAerial.lets_it_drop`): free ball, coming down on him, nobody near, no goal
in front of him, so he waits and chests it. Asked by `SimDuel` *before* he is a
contender, because a declined touch that has already been booked as a recovery is
a lie in the log.

**Strike reach** — how far a player can hit the ball along a given line, as a
fraction of what he could hit it facing that way (`SimTouch.strike_scale`). A ball
played behind the body has no backlift and no hips behind it, so it is not a worse
pass, it is a *shorter* one. The decision layer gates its candidates on it and the
touch primitives clamp the aim point to it, so the long ball behind you is not
available at any accuracy — it has to be turned onto first.

## How an option is judged

Every candidate is a dictionary with the same shape, scored by `score_of` as:

```
success x gain  -  (1 - success) x risk_weight x loss
```

All of it in **goal probability**, which is what makes a shot, a pass and a carry
directly comparable.

**Success** — the chance the thing comes off. Chance of scoring for a shot, of
completion for a pass, of still having the ball for a carry.

**Gain** — what the ball is worth where it ends up if it does come off.

**Loss** — what it costs if it does not: the threat conceded to the opposition at
the point the ball would be lost, which is *where the touch was going*, not where
the player is standing. Read at his own feet it is the same number for every
option, and then nothing can tell dribbling toward his own goal from dribbling
toward the other one.

**Expected threat (xT)** — the chance a goal eventually follows from the ball being
at a given point. A grid, in `SimValueField.xt_at`. Roughly 0.0002 deep in your own
half against 0.38 near the penalty spot: very flat at the back and very steep at
the front, and a lot of the engine's behaviour follows from that shape alone.

**Expected goals (xG)** — the chance a *shot* from here scores. `expected_goals`.

**Pitch control** — the probability your side wins the ball at a given point,
`control_at`. Not "who is nearest": every player's arrival time is computed with the
momentum he has to shed first, and everyone within reach is weighed, so five
defenders around a point read differently from one. `control_at_time` asks the same
question given that the ball takes a known time to arrive.

**Possession value** — `possession_value`. What simply having the ball is worth.
Without it the engine compares only where the ball ends up, and a fifty-metre punt to
a well-placed striker beats a fifteen-metre pass that keeps it. `POSSESSION_VALUE` is
its average over the pitch; `TERRITORY` tilts it up the field, and both are read
through the function, never as the bare constant.

**Territory** — that tilt, and the only thing in the engine that says a metre up the
pitch is worth having. Expected threat is flat at the back — 0.0001 on your own
eighteen-yard line — so without it a pass forward and a pass back scored the same and
`success` picked, which meant the ball went back. Charged twice: what your possession
gains, and what the opponent's would be worth if you lost it there.

**Bias** — a per-candidate multiplier on the *positional value* of an option, where
the tactical plan and the standing penalties live. Never a multiplier on the whole
score, because a penalty applied to a negative number makes a bad option look good.

**Softmax** — how the choice is made, never argmax. The **temperature** falls with
the `decisions` attribute, so a good decision-maker more often takes the genuinely
best option and a weak one makes plausible-but-wrong choices. It is measured against
the *spread* of the candidate scores rather than being absolute, because the whole
list often fits inside a range of 0.02.

**Regain** — the two seconds after winning the ball back. `regain_urgency` decays
over that window, and while it is up the priority is securing the ball rather than
advancing it: beating a man is hardest at the moment you have just taken it off him.

**Uncontrolled** — the ball arrived with real pace and this player did not put it
there. He is taking it down, not choosing from a full menu.

## Room, and the sizes of a touch

Four measurements of "how much space is there", answering four different questions.
Confusing them is how a carrier knocks the ball into touch and calls it a run in
behind.

**`run_room`** — raw distance from a point to the boundary along a direction. The
geometric primitive the others are built from.

**`carry_room`** — how big a touch this direction has room for. Converts the
*intended gap* into the ground the ball actually covers before it slows to the
carrier's pace, then inverts. The ball is struck to be `ahead` metres clear of a man
who keeps running, so in the world frame it travels far further than `ahead`.

**`_room_ahead`** — the same question for the knock past a man, charged against
where the ball would *stop rolling* rather than where he catches it up. He does not
catch this one; that is what makes it a foot race.

**`stride_room`** — how big a touch his own pace this way is *worth*. His speed
along the touch, not his speed: a man sprinting who shifts it square across himself
has none of that pace going where the ball is going, and gets the short touch he
should.

**`close_control`** — shrinks a touch by how near goal it is being played. Four
metres in front of you is a way of covering ground, and inside the penalty area
there is no ground left to cover — the ball has to stay somewhere it can be struck.

**`ahead`** / **`push`** — how far in front of himself the ball is put, in metres of
*relative* gap. `ahead` for a carry, `push` for the knock past a man. This is the
number in the `touch` column of `Under challenge`.

**`space`** — 0 to 1, how much room the chosen direction bought, which is what sizes
the touch. Read off the room in metres and nothing else. Taking it from the
candidate's *score* means the chosen touch is the long one whatever the traffic,
because every term in the score is high for the option about to be picked.

**`max_ahead`** — the cap that guarantees the touch played is the touch that was
scored. A candidate scored on a nine-metre knock and executed as a four-metre touch
is the decision layer lying to itself about its own option.

**Horizon** vs **reach** — the distance a direction is *judged* over against the
distance the ball is *knocked*. Two numbers, deliberately. Size the judgement off the
touch and a jogging carrier asks about the grass 1.5 m in front of his feet, where
there is never a defender and never a touchline.

**`_in_play_odds`** — the probability the touch leaves the ball on the field, given
the aim error. A hard room test cannot express a winger carrying up the touchline: he
has all the grass in the world *along* his direction and none beside it. Priced
rather than forbidden, and the softmax then turns him infield on its own.

**`_escape_value`** — the race between the carrier and the man closing for the
touch's landing point. This is what makes a change of direction a real option. Pitch
control cannot express it, because the carrier is nearest to all of his own probes
and every direction comes back the same.

## Off the ball

**Chase primary / chase support** — who goes for a loose ball and who backs him up.
Assigned in `SimMovement._assign_chasers`, which is also where the **anti-swarm
guard** lives, and nowhere else. **A carrier is chase-primary for his own touch**,
which is the source of several subtle bugs: pacing him at "fast enough to arrive" is
right for running onto a loose ball and wrong for running away from a challenge.

**Recovery run** — a chaser coming from behind a carrier runs *round* him rather than
into the back of him, and does it at pace, because the way round is longer than the
way through. Without it a defender welded to the carrier's back can never reach the
ball.

**Slipstream** — a defender stuck directly behind a running carrier, unable to reach a
ball pushed two metres in front of the man. What the recovery run exists to break, and
only `Chasing the carrier` can see it.

**Shape** — where a player stands when he is doing nothing else: the formation's home
position, slid with the ball and shifted by the tactical plan.

**Offering for the ball** — how a player off the ball makes himself available. Three
ways, chosen and held for a commitment window (`SimOffBall`): **show**, coming to meet
the man on the ball; **space**, drifting into a pocket; **behind**, running past the
last defender. A run in behind only goes when the carrier can actually see it —
otherwise strikers sprint in behind on a metronome.

**Making a run** — a committed off-ball run, held to the metre rather than to a
drifter's tolerance.

**Claim** — the keeper coming for a ball in the air inside his own area
(`SimKeeper._claim_target`). Above head height it is his by right; below it he has
to beat the first attacker by a margin scaled by `command`. He then either holds
it or **punches** it, which is a clearance struck with two fists.

## Contests

**Pressure** — a field, per player: how much company he has, weighted so that an
opponent in front presses harder than one behind. It makes him rush, widening every
error distribution in the touch model. It rates the man on a carrier's back at nearly
nothing, which is why it cannot stand in for the next one.

**Challenge** — how close this player is to being *tackled*, from the closing speed of
whoever is coming. It looks out to `CHALLENGE_SIGHT`, which is far enough to see a man
arriving in time for the carrier to do something about it.

**Challenger** — the specific opponent most likely to be the one who challenges: the
nearest inside challenge sight. Read by the decision layer to score a way out, and by
the movement layer to decide whether the carrier runs or jogs.

**Contest** — two or more players going for a ball nobody owns.

**Challenge (the duel)** — a man going in on somebody who *has* it. Harder than a
contest, harder still from behind, and far more likely to be a foul. `Duels` reports
the two separately.

## Tactics and patterns

**Tactics are priors on the decision function, never behaviour switches.** A plan
shifts what options are worth; it does not add or remove options. The knobs:
`press_intensity`, `press_line`, `line_height`, `tempo`, `directness`, `risk`,
`width`, reaching the scoring through `risk_weight`, `future_discount`,
`retention_bias`, `line_x`, `width_scale` and `focus_at`.

**Pattern** — a named, recognisable move: a trigger plus a nudge, on a long cooldown,
with every firing counted and a success rate reported after the match. Overlap,
third-man run, switch to the far side, press the goal kick, run in behind, underlap,
keeper plays short. Not new behaviour. A pattern that fires two hundred times a match
is not a move, it is a background hum.

## Running the thing

**Clock rate** — match-clock seconds per simulated second. The match clock runs faster
than the world; nothing else changes. `--clock-rate 30` plays all ninety minutes in
three, which is what the 3D view opens at. **Not the same thing as `--minutes 10`**,
which plays the first ten minutes and stops: one changes what a match *is*, the other
samples a rate from an ordinary match. `docs/INVARIANTS.md` has what may scale with it.

**Trace** — positions of the ball and every player, five times a second. What the
positional diagnostics are built on, and the only thing that owes the sim nothing:
**when a counter and the trace disagree, believe the trace.**

**Sanity ranges** vs **the §11 bands** — the two printed tables, and they are read
differently. `CLAUDE.md` says how.

## Words that are not synonyms

**Pressure and challenge.** Pressure is company; challenge is a man arriving to take it
off you. Pressure rates the defender on a carrier's back at nearly zero, so a diagnostic
bucketed on pressure will call that carrier *free*.

**A carry and a hold.** Both are `dribble` touches in the log and they are opposite acts:
one covers ground, one refuses to. `Where the carry went` exists because the log cannot
tell them apart.

**`ahead` and how far the ball travels.** `ahead` is the gap the touch is struck to open
against a man who keeps running. The ball's own journey over the grass is two or three
times that. Every touchline bug in this engine's history has been someone checking the
room against the first number.

**A body and an option.** Counting teammates near the ball says nothing about whether
there is a pass on: a man with a defender in the lane is a pass that gets cut out. `Did
he have a safe pass?` is the block that knows the difference.

**Completed and good.** A side that rolls every ball back to its centre halves completes
95% of them and has done nothing. `xT gained` is the honest measure.
